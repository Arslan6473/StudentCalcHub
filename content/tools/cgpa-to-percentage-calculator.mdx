## How it works

A CGPA (cumulative grade point average) on a 10-point scale is an average of grade points, not a mark. There
is no single universal way to turn it into a percentage — each board or university publishes its own
formula, and the one on your transcript is the official one. Most fall into one of two shapes:

- **A plain multiplier:** percentage = CGPA × multiplier. CBSE's Class X grading scheme uses **× 9.5**
  ("overall indicative percentage of marks = 9.5 × CGPA"). IISc (10-point scale) and Cotton University
  use **× 10**; MIT ADT University, Pune uses **× 9.56**.
- **An offset formula:** percentage = (CGPA − offset) × multiplier. For example, (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 means
  subtract 0.75 from your CGPA, then multiply by 10. Pick the custom option and type in the two numbers
  your university gives.

To go the other way, undo the formula: **CGPA = percentage ÷ multiplier + offset**.

Because each formula has a ceiling, some percentages have no CGPA. With × 9.5, a perfect 10 CGPA gives
95%, so a 97% can't be expressed on that scale.

## Worked example

Your CBSE Class X marksheet shows a **CGPA of 8.6**.

1. CBSE's formula: percentage = CGPA × 9.5.
2. 8.6 × 9.5 = **81.7%**.

Now say your university's notice gives the offset formula (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 and your CGPA is also 8.6:

1. 8.6 − 0.75 = 7.85
2. 7.85 × 10 = **78.5%**

The same CGPA gives two different percentages, which is why you should always use the formula your own
institution publishes.

## When to use this

- To fill in a "percentage" box on a job, scholarship or admission form when your marksheet only shows a CGPA.
- To check that an application form's minimum (for example "60% or equivalent CGPA") is one you meet.
- To work out the CGPA you need for a target percentage, using the % → CGPA tab.
- To compare students from boards or universities that report on different scales — keeping in mind that
  converted figures are only approximate.

## Common mistakes

- **Using × 9.5 for everything.** It is CBSE's Class X rule. Many universities use × 10, × 9.56 or an
  offset formula instead.
- **Converting a CGPA from a different scale.** This tool is for 10-point CGPAs only. An 8-point or 4-point
  CGPA needs that institution's own formula — IISc, for example, multiplies its 8-point CGPA by 12.5.
- **Treating the result as your real marks.** A converted percentage is an equivalent, not the marks you
  scored. If a form needs an official figure, get your university's conversion certificate or notice.
- **Rounding the CGPA first.** Convert the CGPA exactly as printed (8.63, not 8.6), then round the answer.
- **Mixing up SGPA and CGPA.** SGPA is one semester; CGPA covers the whole programme. Convert the one the
  form asks for.
