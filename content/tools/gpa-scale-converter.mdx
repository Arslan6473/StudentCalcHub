## How it works

Countries grade on different scales: a 4.0 GPA in the US, a 7-point GPA in Australia, a 10-point CGPA in
India and Pakistan, marks out of 20 in France, percentages almost everywhere. When you apply abroad, you
often have to state your grade on someone else's scale. This converter offers two methods.

**Linear (proportional) conversion** — between any two of the 4.0, 5.0, 7-point, 10-point, 0–20,
percentage or custom scales. Your grade keeps the same share of its scale:

**new grade = your grade ÷ old maximum × new maximum**

With a custom scale that does not start at 0, the lowest grade is subtracted first, so the bottom of one
scale lines up with the bottom of the other.

**Modified Bavarian formula** — to a German grade, where **1.0 is the best grade and 4.0 is the lowest
pass**. The KMK (the conference of Germany's education ministers) sets this formula in its agreement on
foreign certificates:

**German grade = 1 + 3 × (Nmax − Nd) ÷ (Nmax − Nmin)**

- **Nmax** is the best grade on your scale.
- **Nmin** is the lowest passing grade on your scale.
- **Nd** is your grade.

The KMK rule is to keep **one decimal place without rounding**, so 1.75 becomes 1.7. A grade below your
pass mark comes out worse than 4.0, which is a fail in Germany.

### Every result is an estimate

A formula cannot know how strict your university is. Universities and credential evaluators such as WES
often convert grade by grade with country-specific tables, and German universities send many applicants
through uni-assist, which applies the official values. Use this tool to compare and plan, then check the
method each university or evaluator names on its admissions page.

## Worked example

You have an **8.5 CGPA on a 10-point scale** and are applying to a US master's program.

1. Share of the scale: 8.5 ÷ 10 = 0.85
2. Scale up: 0.85 × 4 = **3.40 on the 4.0 scale**

Now you are also applying in Germany with a French degree average of **14/20**. In France you need an
average of 10/20 to validate credits, so Nmin = 10.

1. German grade = 1 + 3 × (20 − 14) ÷ (20 − 10)
2. = 1 + 3 × 0.6 = **2.8**

The rounding rule matters at the edges. With 85% on a scale where 40% is the pass mark, the formula gives
1 + 3 × 15 ÷ 60 = 1.75, which is cut to **1.7**, not rounded to 1.8.

Converting individual US letter grades instead? Use the [letter grade converter](/gpa/grade-converter).

## When to use this

- To fill in a "GPA on a 4.0 scale" field when your university grades out of 10, 20 or 100, and the form
  allows a self-reported estimate.
- To check whether you meet a German program's grade requirement before applying.
- To compare offers or scholarship cutoffs that are written on a scale you are not used to.
- To see what a grade from an exchange semester means on your home scale.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the estimate as official.** If an application asks for a WES evaluation, a uni-assist
  report or your university's own conversion, that number is the one that counts, and it can differ from
  a linear result.
- **Using the wrong pass mark.** The German result depends heavily on Nmin. 14/20 is 2.8 with a pass mark
  of 10, but 2.5 with a pass mark of 8. Take Nmin from your transcript or university rules.
- **Converting a percentage that came from a CGPA formula.** If your percentage was itself calculated from
  a CGPA, converting it again stacks two approximations. Start from the grade printed on your transcript.
- **Rounding the German grade up.** The KMK rule cuts to one decimal. A 2.29 is a 2.2, not a 2.3.
- **Mixing scales where lower is better.** German grades run the other way: 1.0 is the best. Don't enter
  a German grade as if 4.0 were the top.
