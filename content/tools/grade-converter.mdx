## How it works

Most US high schools and colleges report grades on an **unweighted 4.0 scale**. Each letter grade has a
fixed number of grade points, and each letter covers a band of percentages:

- **Percent → letter:** find the band your percentage falls in. On the common scale, 87% up to (but not
  including) 90% is a B+.
- **Letter → points:** look the letter up. A and A+ are 4.0, A- is 3.7, B+ is 3.3, and each step down
  takes off about 0.3 or 0.4.
- **GPA → letter:** find the highest letter whose points are at or below your GPA. A 3.5 is above a B+
  (3.3) but below an A- (3.7), so it reads as a B+ average.

The percentage bands are the most common ones, not a rule. Your school or teacher may use different
cutoffs, so this tool tells you what a grade *usually* means.

## Worked example

You have an **89.9%** in Chemistry.

1. 89.9 is at least 87 and below 90, so it falls in the **B+** band (87–89%).
2. A B+ is worth **3.3** grade points.

If the class is worth 1 credit, it adds 3.3 × 1 = 3.3 grade points to your GPA total. To see your whole
GPA, add the grade points for every class and divide by your total credits.

## When to use this

- To check what letter a class percentage will turn into on your report card.
- To estimate your GPA from a list of letter grades.
- To understand an unweighted GPA on an application, such as what a 3.5 means in letter terms.
- To compare your grades with the GPA cutoffs for [Latin honors](/gpa/latin-honors) at your college.

## Common mistakes

- **Rounding up without checking.** An 89.5% is a B+ unless your teacher's syllabus says grades round.
- **Mixing weighted and unweighted GPA.** A 4.3 weighted GPA includes honors/AP bonus points. Convert
  each class on the unweighted scale instead.
- **Assuming every school uses the same chart.** Some colleges give A+ a 4.3, some have no plus/minus
  grades, and some start an A at 90%. Your school's own scale always wins.
- **Leaving out credit weights.** A 4-credit class counts four times as much as a 1-credit class in
  your GPA.
