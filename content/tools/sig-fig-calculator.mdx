## How it works

Significant figures ("sig figs") are the digits in a measurement that carry real information. The
calculator applies the standard rules from general chemistry:

- **Nonzero digits** are always significant: 4.56 has 3.
- **Zeros between nonzero digits** are significant: 305 has 3.
- **Leading zeros** are never significant: 0.00450 starts counting at the 4.
- **Trailing zeros after a decimal point** are significant: 100.0 has 4, and 0.00450 has 3.
- **Trailing zeros with no decimal point** are ambiguous: 1500 is counted as 2. Write 1500. or
  1.500 × 10^3 to show that all four were measured.

For scientific notation, only the number before "× 10" counts: 1.50 × 10^3 has 3 sig figs.

When you combine measurements, the answer can't be more precise than your least precise input:

- **Adding or subtracting:** keep the fewest *decimal places*.
- **Multiplying or dividing:** keep the fewest *significant figures*.

## Worked example

You add a 12.11 g sample to 0.3 g of salt.

1. 12.11 + 0.3 = 12.41 on the calculator.
2. 12.11 goes to the hundredths place, but 0.3 only goes to the tenths place.
3. Round to the tenths: the answer is **12.4 g**.

Now find the area of a 2.5 cm × 3.47 cm rectangle. 2.5 × 3.47 = 8.675, and 2.5 has only 2 sig figs, so
the answer is **8.7 cm²**.

## When to use this

- To check how many significant figures a measurement in your lab notebook has.
- To round a calculator answer before you write it in a lab report.
- To combine two measurements and keep the right precision.
- To round a [percent error](/chemistry/percent-error-calculator) result to match your data.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting leading zeros.** 0.0045 has 2 significant figures, not 5.
- **Dropping significant trailing zeros.** If your balance read 2.00 g, write 2.00, not 2. The zeros
  show the precision of the instrument.
- **Mixing up the two rules.** Addition uses decimal places; multiplication uses sig figs.
- **Rounding at every step.** In a multi-step problem, keep extra digits until the end and round once.
- **Limiting by exact numbers.** Counted values and defined conversions don't limit your sig figs.
