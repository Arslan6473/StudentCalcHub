## How it works

Percent error tells you how far your measured (experimental) value is from the accepted value, as a
percentage of the accepted value:

**Percent error = |experimental − accepted| ÷ |accepted| × 100**

1. Subtract the accepted value from your measured value.
2. Take the absolute value, unless your class keeps the sign.
3. Divide by the accepted value.
4. Multiply by 100 to get a percentage.

The result is then rounded. By default the calculator matches the least precise of your two values, which
is how most lab reports handle [significant figures](/chemistry/sig-fig-calculator).

## Worked example

You measure the density of aluminum as **2.81 g/cm³**. The accepted value is **2.70 g/cm³**.

1. 2.81 − 2.70 = 0.11
2. 0.11 ÷ 2.70 = 0.04074
3. 0.04074 × 100 = 4.074%
4. Both values have 3 significant figures, so report **4.07%**.

If your class keeps the sign, the answer is +4.07%: your value was higher than the accepted one.

## When to use this

- To write the error analysis section of a chemistry or physics lab report.
- To compare a measured density, molar mass or boiling point with the textbook value.
- To check how accurate a titration or calorimetry result was.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing by the wrong value.** Always divide by the accepted value, not your measured value.
- **Forgetting to multiply by 100.** 0.046 is a fraction; 4.6% is the percent error.
- **Reporting too many digits.** A percent error of 4.0740741% claims more precision than your data has.
- **Rounding your inputs first.** Use your measured values as recorded, then round only the final answer.
- **Using it when the accepted value is 0.** The formula divides by zero, so use another comparison.
