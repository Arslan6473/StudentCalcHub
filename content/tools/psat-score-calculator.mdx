## How it works

The PSAT/NMSQT has the same structure as the digital SAT: **54 Reading and Writing** questions and **44 Math**
questions, in two adaptive modules each. Each section is scored from **160 to 760**, for a total of **320 to 1520**.

Like the SAT, the PSAT/NMSQT is **multistage adaptive**, so there is no fixed table that turns a number correct
into a score. This calculator uses **our own estimating curve**, drawn as straight lines between these anchor points:

| Share correct | 0% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 75% | 90% | 100% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section score (about) | 160 | 190 | 320 | 480 | 610 | 690 | 760 |

Scores are rounded to the nearest 10, with a likely range of 30 points either side.

### The National Merit Selection Index

National Merit Scholarship Corporation (NMSC) uses the **Selection Index**, not your total score:

**Selection Index = (2 × Reading and Writing + Math) ÷ 10**

It runs from **48 to 228**. Because Reading and Writing counts twice, 10 more points there raise the index by 2,
while 10 more points in Math raise it by 1.

Semifinalist cutoffs are set **by state**, once a year, after the test. The state comparison uses the most
recent cutoffs we could confirm (see the table below). They are past cutoffs; your year's cutoff may be higher or lower.

## Worked example

You get **48 of 54** Reading and Writing questions and **38 of 44** Math questions right.

1. Reading and Writing: 48 ÷ 54 = 89% correct, an estimate of about **680**.
2. Math: 38 ÷ 44 = 86% correct, an estimate of about **670**.
3. Selection Index: (2 × 680 + 670) ÷ 10 = (1,360 + 670) ÷ 10 = **203**.

For the Class of 2027 the lowest state cutoff and the Commended cutoff were both 208, so this student is about
5 index points short. Thirty more points in Reading and Writing would add 6 to the index; the same gain in Math
would add only 3.

## When to use this

- After a PSAT practice test, to see roughly where your Selection Index lands.
- In the fall of junior year, to decide whether National Merit is a realistic goal worth extra prep.
- To see the trade-off between Reading and Writing and Math, since the index weights them differently.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing your total score to the cutoff.** Cutoffs are Selection Index values, not totals.
- **Assuming last year's cutoff is this year's.** Cutoffs move every year, often by several points.
- **Using a sophomore PSAT.** Only the PSAT/NMSQT taken in junior year counts for National Merit.
- **Forgetting the double weight.** A Reading and Writing point is worth twice as much to the index as a Math point.
