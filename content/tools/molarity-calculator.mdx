## How it works

**Molarity** (M) is the concentration of a solution in moles of solute per liter of solution:

**M = moles of solute ÷ liters of solution**

If you start with a mass, convert it to moles first by dividing by the molar mass. The calculator does
this from the formula you type, using IUPAC atomic weights.

For a **dilution**, the moles of solute don't change when you add water, so:

**M₁ × V₁ = M₂ × V₂**

M₁ and V₁ describe the stock solution you measure out. M₂ and V₂ describe the final solution. Give any
three and the calculator solves for the fourth. V₁ and V₂ just need the same unit.

## Worked example

**Making a solution.** You dissolve 5.844 g of NaCl and make it up to 500 mL.

1. Molar mass of NaCl = 22.990 + 35.45 = 58.44 g/mol
2. 5.844 g ÷ 58.44 g/mol = 0.1000 mol
3. 500 mL = 0.500 L, so 0.1000 mol ÷ 0.500 L = **0.200 M**

**Diluting.** How much 2.0 M stock do you need to make 200 mL of 0.50 M?

V₁ = M₂ × V₂ ÷ M₁ = 0.50 × 200 ÷ 2.0 = **50 mL** of stock. Add water up to a total of 200 mL.

## When to use this

- Preparing a solution of known concentration for a lab.
- Diluting a stock solution to a working concentration.
- Checking titration or homework answers that involve concentration.
- Alongside the [grams to moles calculator](/chemistry/grams-to-moles-calculator) when you need the molar
  mass breakdown.

## Common mistakes

- **Using mL instead of L.** Molarity is per liter: 250 mL is 0.250 L.
- **Using the volume of water added.** Molarity uses the *total* solution volume.
- **Swapping M₁ and M₂.** The stock is always more concentrated than the diluted solution.
- **Mixing volume units in M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.** Use the same unit for V₁ and V₂.
- **Forgetting hydrate water in the molar mass.** Weigh and calculate with the formula on the bottle.
