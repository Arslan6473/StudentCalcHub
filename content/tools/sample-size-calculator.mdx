## How it works

A sample size calculation answers one question: how many responses do you need so that your estimate is
within a chosen margin of error, at a chosen confidence level?

**For a proportion** (a survey question with a yes/no or percentage answer):

**n₀ = z² × p(1 − p) ÷ E²**

**For a mean** (an average of a measurement, such as test scores or reaction times):

**n₀ = (z × σ ÷ E)²**

- **z** comes from the confidence level: 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95%, 2.576 for 99%.
- **E** is the margin of error. For a proportion, write it as a decimal (5% = 0.05).
- **p** is the proportion you expect. **σ** is the standard deviation you expect.

If you are sampling from a small, known group, the **finite population correction** lowers the number:
n = n₀ ÷ (1 + (n₀ − 1) ÷ N), where N is the population size. Always round the final answer **up**.

### Why p = 0.5 is the safe default

The term p(1 − p) is 0.25 when p = 0.5, and smaller for any other value: 0.21 at p = 0.3, 0.09 at p = 0.1.
So if you have no earlier estimate, using 50% gives the largest sample size the formula can produce. Whatever
the true proportion is, your margin of error will be no wider than the one you asked for.

## Worked example

Your student council wants to survey a school of **1,200 students** at **95% confidence** with a **±5%**
margin of error. Nobody knows what share will answer yes, so use p = 0.5.

1. z for 95% confidence is 1.96.
2. n₀ = 1.96² × 0.5 × 0.5 ÷ 0.05² = 384.16
3. The school is small, so correct for it: 384.16 ÷ (1 + 383.16 ÷ 1,200) = 291.18
4. Round up: you need **292 responses**.

Without the population correction you would need 385. For a mean instead: at 95% confidence, a margin of
±2 points and a standard deviation of 10 points, n₀ = (1.96 × 10 ÷ 2)² = 96.04, so you need **97**.

## When to use this

- To plan a survey for a class project, AP Statistics assignment or student organization.
- To justify your sample size in the methods section of a research proposal or capstone.
- To check whether a survey you have already run had enough responses for the margin you report.

## Common mistakes

- **Entering the margin as a whole number in the formula.** 5% is 0.05, not 5. The calculator converts it for you.
- **Rounding down.** 384.16 means 385 people; 384 would give a margin slightly wider than you asked for.
- **Counting people you asked, not people who answered.** If you expect a 40% response rate, invite about
  2.5 times the number shown.
- **Using the population correction for a huge population.** It makes almost no difference, and an
  overestimate of N is harmless; leave it blank if unsure.
- **Assuming a big sample removes bias.** The formula covers random error only. A convenience sample of your
  friends stays biased however many you ask.
