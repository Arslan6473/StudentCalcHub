## How it works

The digital SAT has two sections, each scored from **200 to 800** in steps of 10:

- **Reading and Writing:** 54 questions in two modules of 27.
- **Math:** 44 questions in two modules of 22.

Your total score is the two section scores added together, from **400 to 1600**.

The real test does not use a fixed raw-score table. It is **multistage adaptive**: how you do on the first
module decides whether your second module is easier or harder, and College Board scores you using the
difficulty of each question you got right. So there is no official "40 correct = 650" chart for the digital SAT.

This calculator uses **our own estimating curve**. It converts the share of questions you got right into a
section score by drawing straight lines between a few anchor points, chosen to match publicly reported
practice-test results:

| Share correct | 0% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 75% | 90% | 100% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section score (about) | 200 | 230 | 360 | 520 | 650 | 730 | 800 |

The middle value is rounded to the nearest 10. The "likely" range is 30 points either side, because your real
score depends on which module and questions you got.

## Worked example

On a practice test you get **40 of 54** Reading and Writing questions and **32 of 44** Math questions right.

1. Reading and Writing: 40 ÷ 54 = 74% correct, which is just under the 75% anchor, so the estimate is about **650**
   (likely 620–680).
2. Math: 32 ÷ 44 = 73% correct, an estimate of about **640** (likely 610–670).
3. Total: 650 + 640 = **1290**, likely 1230–1350.

## When to use this

- After a paper or third-party practice test that gives you a raw count but no scaled score.
- To set a target, for example "how many more Math questions do I need for 700?"
- To compare your SAT estimate with an [ACT estimate](/test-prep/act-score-calculator) before choosing which test to focus on.

For the most accurate practice score, take a full-length test in College Board's Bluebook app, which scores it
the same way as the real SAT.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the estimate as exact.** Two students with the same number correct can get scores 30–50 points apart on the real test.
- **Counting only one module.** Add both modules of a section together before entering your count.
- **Using an old paper SAT table.** The pre-2024 SAT had different sections and question counts, so its tables do not apply.
- **Leaving questions blank.** There is no penalty for wrong answers, so always guess.
