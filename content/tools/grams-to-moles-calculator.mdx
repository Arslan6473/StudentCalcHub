## How it works

A **mole** is a counting unit for particles: 6.02214076 × 10²³ of them (Avogadro's number). The
**molar mass** of a substance is the mass of one mole of it, in grams per mole (g/mol).

### How to find molar mass

1. Read the formula and count the atoms of each element. Numbers after parentheses or brackets
   multiply everything inside: Ca(OH)₂ has 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.
2. Look up each element's atomic mass. This calculator uses the IUPAC abridged standard atomic
   weights (H = 1.0080, C = 12.011, O = 15.999, and so on).
3. Multiply each atomic mass by its count and add the results.

### Converting

- **Grams → moles:** divide by the molar mass. moles = grams ÷ (g/mol)
- **Moles → grams:** multiply by the molar mass. grams = moles × (g/mol)
- **Moles → particles:** multiply by Avogadro's number.

## Worked example

How many moles are in **36.03 g of water**?

1. Molar mass of H₂O: 2 × 1.0080 + 1 × 15.999 = **18.015 g/mol**
2. 36.03 g ÷ 18.015 g/mol = **2.000 mol**
3. 2.000 mol × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules**

For copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate, CuSO₄·5H₂O, the five waters count too:
63.546 + 32.06 + 4 × 15.999 + 5 × 18.015 = **249.68 g/mol**.

## When to use this

- Stoichiometry problems, where you have to turn a mass into moles before using the balanced equation.
- Weighing out a reagent for a lab. Find the grams you need for a target number of moles.
- Finding the molar mass of a formula, including hydrates and complex ions like K₄[Fe(CN)₆].
- Before a [molarity calculation](/chemistry/molarity-calculator) when you know the grams of solute.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong capitalization.** CO (carbon monoxide) and Co (cobalt) are different. So are NO and No.
- **Forgetting the water in a hydrate.** CuSO₄·5H₂O weighs much more per mole than CuSO₄.
- **Not distributing a subscript.** In Ca(OH)₂ the 2 applies to both O and H.
- **Multiplying instead of dividing.** Grams → moles divides by molar mass; moles → grams multiplies.
- **Rounding atomic masses too early.** Keep the table values and round only the final answer to the
  right [significant figures](/chemistry/sig-fig-calculator).
