There is no single correct way to convert a GPA from one country to another. Most universities want your transcript on its original scale, and they convert it themselves using their own rules or an official evaluation service. Your job is to understand roughly where you stand, follow each application's instructions exactly, and never present your own conversion as official.

## Why grading scales don't line up

Every country grades differently, and the differences go deeper than the size of the numbers. Some scales count up (higher is better), some count down, and the pass mark sits in a different place on each one.

| Country | Scale | Best grade | Lowest pass | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | 0.0–4.0 GPA | 4.0 (some schools give A+ a 4.3) | Usually D (1.0) | Credit-weighted average of letter grades |
| United Kingdom | Degree classes from percentage marks | First (usually 70%+) | Third (usually 40%) | A 2:1 (60–69%) is a strong result |
| India | CGPA, often out of 10 | 10 | Varies by university | Some boards publish a percentage formula |
| Germany | 1.0–5.0 | 1.0 | 4.0 | Lower is better; 5.0 is a fail |
| Australia | Often a 7-point GPA | 7 (High Distinction) | 4 (Pass) | Not every university uses 7 points |
| France | 0–20 | 20 | 10 | 14 is already a good mark |

The point of this table is not the exact numbers. It is that a "70" or a "14" or a "2.0" means completely different things depending on where it was earned. A 2.0 is a weak result in the US and a good one in Germany.

## Why a simple linear conversion can mislead

The quickest conversion is linear: divide your mark by the maximum, then multiply by the new maximum. A French 14/20 becomes 14 ÷ 20 × 4 = 2.8 on a 4.0 scale.

That 2.8 looks average to a US reader. But in France a 14 is a good mark. Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne publishes an approximate conversion table for its own transcripts that puts 14–15.9 at a US 4.0. The linear method and the university's own table are more than a full grade point apart.

The same problem happens in reverse. A UK mark of 65% is a solid 2:1, but 65% of 4.0 is 2.6, which a US reader would see as a C+ average.

Linear conversion is fine as a rough first look. It is not what admissions offices use, and you should not write it on an application as your "GPA".

## The modified Bavarian formula (Germany)

German universities often convert foreign grades with the **modified Bavarian formula**. The Technical University of Munich (TUM), Hochschule Osnabrück and the University of Passau all name it on their admissions pages, and TUM notes that it follows a resolution of the Kultusministerkonferenz (the conference of German education ministers).

**German grade = 1 + 3 × (Nmax − Nd) ÷ (Nmax − Nmin)**

- **Nmax** is the best possible grade on your home scale.
- **Nmin** is the lowest passing grade on your home scale.
- **Nd** is your own average.

The result runs from 1.0 (your grade equals the maximum) to 4.0 (your grade equals the pass mark). Hochschule Osnabrück rounds it to one decimal place.

### Worked examples

**Indian CGPA of 8.0 out of 10, pass mark 4.0:**

1. Nmax − Nd = 10 − 8.0 = 2.0
2. Nmax − Nmin = 10 − 4.0 = 6.0
3. 2.0 ÷ 6.0 = 0.333
4. 1 + 3 × 0.333 = **2.0**

**The same CGPA if your university's pass mark is 5.0:**

1. 10 − 8.0 = 2.0
2. 10 − 5.0 = 5.0
3. 2.0 ÷ 5.0 = 0.4
4. 1 + 3 × 0.4 = **2.2**

The pass mark matters a lot. That is why German universities ask for your institution's grading scale, and why you should state the official pass mark clearly in your documents.

**Australian GPA of 5.5 on a 7-point scale, pass mark 4:**

1 + 3 × (7 − 5.5) ÷ (7 − 4) = 1 + 3 × 1.5 ÷ 3 = **2.5**

**French average of 14/20, pass mark 10:**

1 + 3 × (20 − 14) ÷ (20 − 10) = 1 + 3 × 6 ÷ 10 = **2.8**

Notice that the formula only uses the top and the pass mark. It assumes your scale is spread evenly between them, which, as the French example shows, is not always true. TUM describes its own converter as a non-binding first estimate. The official grade is set during the application review.

### Who does the official conversion in Germany

Many German universities use **uni-assist**, a service that checks international applicants' certificates on their behalf. Some universities review applications themselves. In both cases, the conversion you calculate is only for your own planning. You can try it with the [GPA scale converter](/gpa/gpa-scale-converter), which includes the modified Bavarian formula.

## Official credential evaluations (US and Canada)

Many US and Canadian universities ask international applicants for a credential evaluation from an independent service. Two widely used ones are **World Education Services (WES)** and **Educational Credential Evaluators (ECE)**.

- **WES** says that if you need a calculated GPA, you should apply for its Course-by-Course evaluation. Its free iGPA calculator is "for informational purposes only" and isn't available for every country.
- **ECE** offers a Course by Course report, which it describes as its most-ordered report type.

These services use their own country-specific grade tables, so their result can differ from any formula you run yourself. Before you pay for one, check which service (if any) each university accepts. Some universities do the evaluation in-house and do not want an outside report.

## What admissions offices actually want

Admissions staff see transcripts from dozens of countries. In almost every case they want:

1. **Your official transcript** in its original grading scale, with an official translation if it is not in the language they ask for.
2. **Your institution's grading key**: the maximum grade, the pass mark, and what each band means. This is often printed on the back of the transcript.
3. **Your degree certificate** or proof of expected graduation.
4. **An evaluation report**, but only if the university asks for one, and only from the service it names.

What they usually do **not** want is your own conversion typed into a box as if it were official. If an application form asks you to enter a GPA, read its instructions. Some forms ask for your grade on the original scale. Some ask you to leave the field blank if your school doesn't use a 4.0 scale.

## Step by step: converting your GPA for an application

1. **Find your official numbers.** Write down your average exactly as it appears on your transcript, plus the scale's maximum and pass mark. Use your institution's figures, not a general national one.
2. **Check each university's international admissions page.** Many list entry requirements by country, for example "a 2:1 or equivalent" or "a minimum CGPA of 7.5". If your country is listed, compare yourself with that number directly. No conversion needed.
3. **Find out who converts.** Is it the university itself, uni-assist, WES, ECE or another service? Follow that route.
4. **Estimate, if you need to.** Use the method the destination uses: the modified Bavarian formula for Germany, or the destination university's published table if it has one. For an Indian CGPA, check whether your board or university publishes its own percentage formula; the [CGPA to percentage calculator](/gpa/cgpa-to-percentage-calculator) covers CBSE's ×9.5 rule and custom formulas.
5. **Label it as an estimate.** In a personal statement or email, write "approximately 2.5 on the German scale (estimate using the modified Bavarian formula)" rather than presenting it as your grade.
6. **Submit the original.** Upload the official transcript and grading key exactly as requested.

If you are coming from the UK, the [UK degree classification calculator](/gpa/uk-degree-classification-calculator) helps you work out which class your module marks point to. Many universities abroad list a UK class directly in their requirements.

## Common mistakes

- **Converting a UK percentage linearly.** 65% is a 2:1, not a "2.6 GPA".
- **Using the wrong pass mark.** In the Bavarian formula, a pass mark of 4.0 instead of 5.0 can shift the result by 0.2 or more.
- **Mixing up the direction of the scale.** In Germany, 1.0 is the best grade and higher numbers are worse.
- **Paying for the wrong evaluation.** WES only gives a calculated GPA in its Course-by-Course evaluation. Check what the university asks for before ordering.
- **Treating a free calculator as official.** Even WES says its own iGPA calculator is for information only.
- **Rounding up.** If your average is 7.46, write 7.46. Universities check transcripts.
